How to use
  1. Choose Light, Eigenmodes, or Rounded cube.
  2. Click keys or type their QWERTY letters. Hold a key to build intensity; combine keys to mix patterns.
  3. Sound is on by default; use the Sound button to mute it. Sustain keeps released keys active; Clear or Escape resets them.
  4. In Eigenmodes, choose Colored nodes, Vibration strength, or Moving field. Drag the rounded cube to rotate it.
  5. Try a preset or one of the ten songs below the keyboard. Use Pause or Stop to interrupt playback.

∑ A little math. A lot of color.HOW IT WORKS +

01 / Numbers become spectra

Each key has a distinct prime pᵢ and a fixed wavelength λᵢ. A press starts at exponent 1; holding adds 1 at the selected interval, up to 32. Each exponent is an equal-radiant-power weight, not a photon count.

n = ∏ pᵢeᵢ
Sλ(λ) = ∑ eᵢ δ(λ − λᵢ)

Unique factorization recovers every spectral weight. Multiplying numbers adds their spectra, within the instrument’s range. The number 1 means no light. Sustain keeps released weights; pressing again adds to them.

02 / Spectra become perception

Light frequencies are logarithmically spaced from 700 nm red to 400 nm violet, using f = c/λ. The official CIE 1931 2° observer functions convert the sum of spectral lines to XYZ:

(X, Y, Z) = ∑ eᵢ (x̄(λᵢ), ȳ(λᵢ), z̄(λᵢ))

The 1 nm data is linearly interpolated. Mixing adds power at existing frequencies; it does not average frequencies or produce a new spectral line. Different spectra can look identical: a color alone cannot recover the number.

03 / Perception meets a screen

XYZ is transformed to linear sRGB. Most pure spectral colors are outside a screen’s gamut. We clip negative channels, apply one shared exposure-compression scale, then apply the sRGB transfer function. The preview is an approximation; the XYZ values above remain unmodified.

Doubling every exponent doubles XYZ and preserves chromaticity. Equal power does not mean equal perceived brightness, so violet and deep red can look dim. Keycap colors are normalized as a guide.

Sound uses the same exponents as power weights, with one equal-tempered sine tone per prime, C3 to C♯5. Amplitude grows as √eᵢ, because power is proportional to amplitude squared. Tones add before output compression and limiting; these protect loud chords and do not change the encoded spectrum.

s(t) = A₀ ∑ √eᵢ sin(2πfᵢt + φᵢ)

04 / The same weights become modes

Each prime also selects an orthonormal square-plate mode φᵢ. Its amplitude is √eᵢ, so average squared displacement grows linearly with the prime exponent. Colored nodes add drawings of the individual nodal lines; vibration strength shows their squared amplitudes; the moving field shows their signed sum.

u(x, y, t) = ∑ √eᵢ φᵢ(x, y) cos(2πfᵢt)
⟨u²⟩ = ½ ∑ eᵢ φᵢ(x, y)²

The spatial shapes are exact eigenfunctions of an ideal simply supported square plate. Their time frequencies are retuned to the keyboard’s musical notes and slowed 200× for the display. The strength view is mean squared displacement, not physical energy. Colored nodal overlays are not a simulation of sand. The prime number encodes weights, not phase or animation time.
